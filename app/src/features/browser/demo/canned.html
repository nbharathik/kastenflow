Notes that outlast the app that made them

By the editors · 6 min read

Most of what we write down is meant for later. A decade on, the tool is gone, the format is locked away, and the notes are with it. The ones that survive are plain files in a folder you own.

Plain text is boring on purpose. It opens everywhere, it diffs cleanly, and a copy of it can sit in another city without anyone's permission. Version history turns that copy into a record of how your thinking changed.

Keep the files. Everything else is a view of them.

A backup is not a feature to switch on once; it is a habit the tool makes cheap. Private by default, never overwritten, and easy to restore on a new computer.
